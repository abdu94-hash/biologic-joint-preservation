      B1 · Patient information to discuss with your healthcare professional

      PRP: making your treatment choice

      What is PRP?

      Platelet-rich plasma is prepared from a sample of your blood and injected into the treatment area. Preparations differ. Your clinician should explain the product and procedure being proposed.

      What might it do?

      Some patients report less pain or better function, but useful benefit is not guaranteed. PRP has not been established as a way to rebuild your joint cartilage, cure osteoarthritis or prevent a future joint replacement.

      What are the alternatives?

      Depending on your situation, options may include education, tailored exercise, weight-management support, medicines, another injection or specialist assessment. Ask how each option fits the activity you want to improve.

      What should I understand before deciding?

      Ask about pain or swelling after treatment, bruising, bleeding, infection and other risks relevant to you. Discuss your medicines, planned rehabilitation, follow-up and who to contact if symptoms concern you.

      Your decision record

      Check your understanding

      Can you explain what PRP might help, what it cannot promise, and which alternatives you could choose? You may ask for more time before deciding.

      B2 · Keep this page with your individual aftercare plan

      After PRP: your plan and when to get help

      Medicines

      Follow the written plan from your treating team. Do not stop prescribed aspirin, blood thinners or other medicines on your own. Ask the team if the instructions are unclear or your pain relief is inadequate.

      Activity

      Follow the activity and rehabilitation plan agreed for you. Ask when and how to increase activity. A fixed calendar cannot account for every patient's symptoms and function.

      Get urgent assessment at any time

      Seek urgent help for severe or increasing pain, a hot swollen joint, inability to bear weight, fever or shaking chills, spreading redness or discharge. These symptoms need assessment even on the first day. No fever does not mean there is no infection. Do not wait until a routine follow-up appointment.

      For collapse, breathing difficulty or other emergency symptoms, contact local emergency services. If you cannot reach your clinic about urgent joint symptoms, use the urgent-care route below.

      If the treatment does not help

      Tell the team at review, or sooner if symptoms worsen. The next step should include reassessment and discussion of alternatives. Another injection is not an automatic next step.
